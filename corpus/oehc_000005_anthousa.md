[col. 597-8 l.49] Anthousa, daughter of King Constantine Kopronymus.Having been forced by her father many times to be married to a man, she was not convinced;Wherefore, after his death, having received permission, all her qualifications were to her poverty, and to churches and sacred houses

[insert from Men.Basil. PG 117: 409B: ... From the market of the captives, and her golden garments are the beautification of the churches... end insert]

distributed, the father of many orphans [lege: mother] [

insert from Men.Basil. PG 117: 409B:

... For when she gathered up the fallen children, she nurtured and educated;And he referred to God when he had died, and he arranged the living to old people's homes...end insert]

and widows' protector and guardians.And many exhortations, being hastened by Eurine the Orthodox Auguste, to agree with her and to reign with her, she did not endure.And during the days he spent in the palace, on the outside a royal robe was clothed, and on the inside was a rag of hair.Her drink of water, her ascetic food, the tear in her eyes, and the divine hymn unceasing in her mouth.Wherefore the monastic also underwent life, by the hand of the saint Tarasius, who was buried in the monastery of Omonia

[variant from col.614 l.11: in the monastery of Eumenia].

And from that time on, neither this one of the monastery nor any other of the brethren went out.He was never absent from the church
[col. 599-600 l.35] She did not slacken in prayer, she did not cease her tears, her humility was immeasurable, her diligence in the ministry of the brethren was exceeding, and her diligence in the adoration of the holy temple was exceedingly admirable, and everything else was of the best word and miracle.
